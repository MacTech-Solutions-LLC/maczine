Freehold: secure comms you hold outright.

No server, no accounts, no per-seat rent, no third party holding your metadata — and it keeps working where the internet doesn't reach. A field report on the tool, and on the discipline that makes it credible.

By MacTech SolutionsReading time · 7 minfreehold · v1.0 · MIT

Every small defense contractor we work with has the same conversation problem. The program traffic that actually runs the shop — "the crucible pour moved to Friday," "QA hold on lot 47," a 300 MB fixture model — lives somewhere between text messages that violate the records rules and a cloud messenger subscription that costs real money, holds your metadata, and stops working the moment the network does.

For a ten-person sub, the cloud option runs roughly $1,800 a year with retention locked in a premium tier — and cross-org collaboration means admin-gated federation, negotiated pair by pair.

Freehold is the third option: comms your team owns — like the shop floor, like the machines on it. Peer-to-peer encrypted chat, calls and file transfer; your identity is a cryptographic key generated on your machine; peers connect directly, NAT holepunching included.

01

Sovereign by architecture

There is nothing to breach, subpoena, or bill you, because there is no vendor infrastructure at all. Every link runs the Noise protocol with an independent hybrid post-quantum layer (ML-KEM-768, FIPS 203) inside it — a fresh key for every message, periodic re-keying, downgrade refusal. Traffic recorded today stays unreadable to tomorrow's computers — ahead of CNSA 2.0 timelines, and of where the incumbent cloud messengers publicly are.

Team trust is a roster signed by your team lead's key. Policy — retention, expiry caps, marking banners, mandatory at-rest encryption — is enforced by every member's machine, not suggested by an admin console. A sub on three programs follows three rosters at once; where they overlap, the strictest policy wins.

"Relayed provenance is proof, not trust — a tampered message is refused outright, and the refusal lands in the audit chain."

— the property that makes store-and-forward safe
02

Where clouds can't go

Two laptops on one network segment find each other in under a minute — no configuration, no internet, an identity-free beacon. Messages relay through teammates to people you never overlap with, signatures intact, and 2 GB chunked transfers survive a dropped link and a full restart — resuming where they stopped, hash-verified on both ends.

03

Compliance without a cloud

This is the part that matters at assessment time. One command exports a signed evidence bundle — verified audit chain, roster policy, retention settings, crypto inventory — mapped to NIST SP 800-171 control families and verifiable offline by a third party with nothing but Node.js. Drop it in an SSP; hand it to a C3PAO.

Records channels resolve the retention-versus-ephemerality tension the DoD IG has documented, instead of dodging it: roster policy declares a channel append-only, deletion and self-destruct are refused on every member's machine, and signed, timestamped transcripts export on demand — while everything outside records channels stays as ephemeral as the team wants. CUI marking banners are enforced on both ends.

Even the traffic-metadata claims ship with a review procedure: the app states exactly what a network observer can and cannot see, and includes a packet-capture verifier so a reviewer who does not trust the claim can check it against real bytes.

04

A real daily driver

None of this would matter if the tool lost to convenience. The web UI is a full Slack-style client — channels, threads, reactions, editing, unread badges, @mentions, desktop notifications, search, voice and video calls, screen sharing — plus a complete terminal client. One identity spans up to eight machines; a stolen laptop is revoked account-wide and stops receiving ciphertext entirely. At rest, an optional vault encrypts everything under a passphrase-derived key, with idle auto-relock, a duress passphrase, and a dead-man switch for abandoned machines.

05

Five minutes, no IT dept.

1

Double-click

Node.js is the only prerequisite. start-ui.command (macOS) or start-ui.bat (Windows) installs and opens the app.

2

Swap UIDs once

Send yours over any channel you already trust; add theirs. Verify with matching emoji fingerprints on your first call.

3

Operational

Direct encrypted links come up on their own — no port forwarding, no firewall tickets, no server. One roster file is the whole admin story.

Every capability in this report is enforced by the protocol and proven by an automated end-to-end gate — 21 test suites that spawn real nodes over the real network and try to break the claim: a carrier who alters a relayed message is refused; a tampered evidence bundle fails verification; a 1 GB transfer killed at 30% resumes byte-identical; padded messages of different lengths are byte-identical on the wire. That discipline, more than any single feature, is the product. ◆

◆ What it does NOT claim
  • No IL4/IL5, no FedRAMP — a records-honest complement to accredited tools, never a substitute. Confirm CUI use with your FSO.
  • Program-team scale: tens of people, not thousands of seats.
  • Desktop today; no mobile client yet.
  • No magic: a compromised laptop beats any messenger, and a forgotten vault passphrase has no recovery — nobody else holds your keys.
◆ vs. renting a messenger

  • Infrastructure — none vs vendor cloud holding metadata

  • Air-gapped — native vs no cloud, no service
  • Post-quantum — on every link today vs no public evidence
  • Evidence — signed 800-171 bundle vs the vendor's own paperwork
